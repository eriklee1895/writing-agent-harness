# Cultural Essay Style

中文名：文化随笔

## Intent

`cultural-essay` is for personal essays about cultural phenomena, cities, music, travel, literary associations, and everyday discoveries with a cultural layer. It sits between `literary-essay` and analytical commentary: lighter and more textured than a tech essay, but with more structure and public-topic clarity than a pure personal prose piece.

## Visual System

- White background: keep the article compatible with phone dark/night reading behavior; avoid warm paper backgrounds that stay too bright at night.
- Jade green headings: connects to cultural topics and city/history writing without using loud red. H2 uses a bold title-serif treatment so section titles remain clear without becoming product-dashboard labels.
- Bronze accent: used sparingly for hero label, heading borders, inline emphasis, and quote borders.
- Serif-first typography: supports a slower, essay-like reading rhythm.
- Subtle highlight cards: render standalone `==重点句==` as restrained Notion/Feishu-like callout cards for selected thesis sentences.
- Minimal cards: body sections stay open and breathable; avoid turning the article into a dashboard.

## Structure

1. Hero: left-aligned, white background, small bronze rule + `文化随笔` label, H1, and optional deck.
2. No outline by default. This style is meant to preserve essay flow.
3. Body: full-width open sections, no card containers.
4. H2: light jade highlight block with a short bronze left border and bold title-serif text. Do not use decorative prefix symbols.
5. Quote blocks: warm paper-toned blocks with bronze left border.
6. Image blocks: full-width figure with small caption; no double frames.
7. No dark closing CTA. Let the essay's final line close the piece.

## Use When

- Personal cultural essays.
- Music + short-video + city/cultural tourism observations.
- Literary travel notes or city essays.
- Articles where humor and cultural judgment should feel calm, not like a hot-take layout.

## Avoid

- Technical explainers or AI infrastructure commentary: use `impact-rational` or `tech-blog`.
- Pure lyric/prose pieces with almost no public-topic analysis: use `literary-essay`.
- Saturated guochao poster styling. The renderer should feel restrained; visual drama should come from article images, not decorative layout.
- Overusing highlights. Mark only a few sentences that carry real judgment or turn the essay's direction.
